Preprint AI Ethics · Wittgenstein
Algorithms Do Not Decide: Responsibility, Hinge Structures, and the Mislocation of Agency in AI Systems
Zenodo — 10.5281/zenodo.xxxxxx · 2024

This paper argues that the attribution of decision-making and moral agency to algorithmic systems constitutes a systematic philosophical error with significant ethical and governance consequences. Drawing on Wittgenstein's concept of hinge propositions from On Certainty, I develop the notion of "hinge inheritance" — the process by which foundational assumptions migrate from designers to deployed systems. The paper contends that algorithms instantiate prior human judgment without being answerable to the normative practices that make responsibility attribution meaningful.

Working Paper Wittgenstein · Epistemology
Hinge Propositions and Epistemic Foundations in Machine Learning Systems
Working Manuscript · 2024

An extended treatment of the epistemological dimensions of hinge inheritance, examining how the foundational assumptions embedded in ML training pipelines function analogously to Wittgensteinian hinge propositions — as conditions of possibility for the system's operation rather than claims the system can evaluate.

Working Paper AI Ethics · Philosophy of Action
On the Moral Grammar of Automated Systems: A Wittgensteinian Critique
Working Manuscript · 2023

An investigation of the language we use to describe AI behavior, arguing that our ordinary moral vocabulary — deciding, choosing, judging — when applied to automated systems, imports unwarranted assumptions about agency and thereby obscures rather than clarifies our ethical situation.
