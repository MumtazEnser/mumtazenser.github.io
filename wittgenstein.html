On Certainty
OC · 1951
OC · §341

"The questions that we raise and our doubts depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, are as it were like hinges on which those turn."

The hinge proposition is not a claim we have justified but one we have absorbed — it is the axis around which inquiry rotates. Applied to algorithmic systems: the foundational assumptions embedded in training data and objective functions function precisely as hinges. They are not examined; they enable examination.

Hinge Propositions Epistemic Foundations AI Applications
OC · §204

"Giving grounds, however, justifying the evidence, comes to an end; — but the end is not certain propositions striking us immediately as true, i.e. it is not a kind of seeing on our part; it is our acting, which lies at the bottom of the language-game."

The regress of justification terminates not in axiomatic truths but in practice. This is why algorithms cannot bear moral responsibility: responsibility requires the capacity to engage in — and be answerable to — the form of life that grounds the language-game of accountability.

Language Games Justification Responsibility
PI · §201

"There is a way of grasping a rule which is not an interpretation, but which is exhibited in what we call 'obeying the rule' and 'going against it' in actual cases."

The rule-following paradox destabilizes the assumption that algorithms follow rules in any philosophically robust sense. Rule-following, for Wittgenstein, is embedded in a social practice — in training, correction, shared reaction. The algorithm instantiates a pattern; whether it follows a rule is a question about the form of life that surrounds it, not about the system itself.

The Architecture of Hinge Structures

My research applies Wittgenstein's late epistemology to problems in AI ethics. The central claim: algorithmic systems inherit hinge propositions from their designers and training corpora without the capacity to interrogate them — and this inheritance is precisely what makes the attribution of agency to such systems a philosophical error.

What appears as machine judgment is the crystallization of prior human judgment, embedded in weights and architectures that are themselves products of forms of life the machine cannot access.

Research Notes — Ongoing
Hinge Structures in Neural Networks

Training objectives, architectural choices, and data curation decisions function as hinge propositions for machine learning systems — foundational assumptions that are never examined within the system's own operation.

The Form of Life Argument

Genuine participation in the language-game of moral accountability requires embeddedness in a form of life. Algorithmic outputs can be assessed by that language-game but cannot participate in it as agents.

Beyond the Turing Frame

The Wittgensteinian critique suggests the Turing Test asks the wrong question. Behavioral indistinguishability is insufficient for agency attribution — the question is about the form of life, not the performance.
