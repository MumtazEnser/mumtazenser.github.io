Research Statement

Mumtaz Enser

Wittgenstein Researcher working on philosophy of AI, responsibility, normative structures, technology ethics, institutional decision systems, and algorithmic accountability.

My work investigates how technological systems appear to decide while human and institutional practices continue to authorize, interpret, implement, and normalize their outputs.

Instead of treating the “responsibility gap” as a real absence, I analyze it as a conceptual misdescription of distributed practices.

Theoretical Architecture

Key Concepts

These are no longer presented as “under review” items. They are the conceptual nodes of the research program.

Authority Illusion

Authority illusion names the misrecognition of institutional and collective authority as if it originated from the artificial system itself. AI systems do not possess normative authority; institutions project authority onto outputs and then treat them as objective, neutral, or inevitable.

AI GovernanceAuthorityNormativity

Ethical Withdrawal

Ethical withdrawal describes a condition in which visible harm ceases to function as a practical interruption because procedural correctness replaces ethical judgment. Reason remains; judgment withdraws.

EthicsJudgmentDelegation

Protean Responsibility

Responsibility does not disappear inside AI systems; it changes form. It disperses across design choices, data practices, model architecture, deployment, institutional interpretation, and practical implementation.

Distributed ResponsibilityInstitutions

Algorithms Do Not Decide

Algorithms produce outputs. Decisions arise only when those outputs are interpreted, authorized, and enacted within a normative practice. The phrase “algorithmic decision-making” obscures this institutional transformation.

DecisionPracticeAccountability

Responsibility Without Gaps

Responsibility is relational, not locational. No single node holds responsibility alone, but responsibility also does not vanish. Where there is an effect within a practice, there is a relation; where there is a relation, there is responsibility.

Relational TheoryAI Systems

Antagonism, Dependence, and Domination

Human relations are simultaneously structured by dependence, antagonism, and unequal distributions of power. Social order emerges through vulnerability, negotiation, instability, and structural asymmetry.

Normative Closure and Political Intelligibility

Normative Closure and Political Intelligibility

This article argues that normative closure should not be understood primarily as censorship, exclusion, or communicative distortion, but as a transformation in the conditions under which critique itself becomes intelligible.

Drawing on Wittgenstein’s later philosophy and hinge commitments, the paper develops an account of political intelligibility in which certain challenges fail to register as meaningful questions at all.
