Abstract

Normative closure should not be understood merely as censorship, exclusion, or communicative distortion. It names a transformation in the conditions of intelligibility themselves, where certain questions cease to appear as meaningful possibilities. Drawing on Wittgenstein’s later philosophy and hinge commitments, this framework argues that critique often fails not because it is suppressed, but because it no longer registers as intelligible criticism within an already stabilized normative order.

Conceptual Function

This concept is part of a broader framework concerning AI governance, institutional responsibility, Wittgensteinian critique, and the conditions under which judgment remains visible or withdraws from practice.
