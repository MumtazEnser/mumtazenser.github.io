“An output is not yet a decision.”

Abstract

Algorithms do not make decisions in the normative sense required for responsibility, judgment, or accountability. Outputs become socially effective only through institutional interpretation and authorization. The language of algorithmic decision-making obscures the human practices that transform computational outputs into actionable institutional realities.

Conceptual Function

This concept is part of a broader framework concerning AI governance, institutional responsibility, Wittgensteinian critique, and the conditions under which judgment remains visible or withdraws from practice.
