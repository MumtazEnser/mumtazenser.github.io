Wittgenstein & Metaphilosophy

Questioning, Closure, and Philosophical Method

“I am sitting with a philosopher in the garden; he says again and again ‘I know that that's a tree’, pointing to a tree that is near us... We are only doing philosophy.”

— Ludwig Wittgenstein, On Certainty, §467

This research line investigates how philosophical inquiry becomes possible, structured, and limited through practices of questioning, conceptual closure, and intelligibility. Drawing primarily on Wittgenstein, while engaging Kant and Heidegger, these works examine rule-following, certainty, disagreement, and the conditions under which philosophical problems emerge, persist, or become suspended.

Philosophical problems do not merely arise from error, but from the conditions through which questioning itself becomes possible or restricted.
Research Areas

Core Problems

Rule-Following

How practices acquire intelligibility and how meaning emerges through socially shared forms of life.

Certainty & Closure

The conditions under which questioning becomes suspended, stabilized, or rendered unintelligible.

Disagreement

Persistent disagreement not as informational deficiency, but as divergence across epistemic and conceptual strata.

Philosophical Method

Philosophy as diagnosis, clarification, and conceptual reorientation rather than theoretical system-building.
