01

Algorithms Do Not Decide

This paper argues that algorithms do not make decisions but generate outputs that become decisions only through human practices. By distinguishing outputs from judgments, the paper reframes the so-called “responsibility gap” as a category mistake grounded in the mislocation of responsibility within socio-technical systems.

02

Authority Illusion

This paper introduces the concept of authority illusion to explain how responsibility becomes structurally displaced within socio-technical systems. The paper develops an authority mapping framework showing how institutional structures obscure the relational distribution of responsibility.

authority illusion · distributed responsibility · AI ethics · institutional accountability

03

Responsibility Without Gaps

This paper develops a relational theory of responsibility for socio-technical systems by rejecting individualistic models of agency. Responsibility is reconceived as distributed across interconnected roles, epistemic structures, and organizational forms of life.

relational responsibility · distributed agency · epistemic responsibility · institutional practices
Read Paper →
04

The Responsibility Gap Is a Category Mistake

This paper argues that the so-called responsibility gap arises from a category mistake: responsibility is mislocated at the level of system outputs rather than within the practices that interpret and enact them. The problem is not the absence of responsibility, but its systematic misrecognition.

responsibility gap · authority illusion · ethical withdrawal · socio-technical systems
